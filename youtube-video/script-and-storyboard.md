# Your Next Step Starts Here — script and storyboard

**Planned length:** 4:20<br>
**Format:** 16:9, 1920×1080<br>
**Audience:** YESS students, families, educators, and support staff<br>
**Tone:** hopeful, practical, respectful, and non-judgmental

## 0:00–0:15 — Opening

**Picture:** Begin on the My Next Step – YESS home page. Slowly move the pointer toward the heading and the two main buttons. Do not click yet.

**On-screen text:**

> YOUR NEXT STEP STARTS HERE<br>
> A guide for YESS students

**Narration:**

> Your future does not have to be figured out today. You only need a next step. My Next Step – YESS is a website that helps you find that step, whether you are returning to school, earning credits, waiting for placement, or planning what comes after high school.

## 0:15–0:43 — Start where you are

**Picture:** Move across the “Where are you right now?” choices. Pause on “I already enrolled,” “I'm waiting for placement,” “I don't know yet,” and “I need my credits.”

**On-screen text:**

> Start with what you need today.

**Narration:**

> Start on the home page and choose the situation that sounds most like you. You can get help after enrolling, use your time while waiting, explore choices when you are not sure, check credits, request records, or plan for work and education. There is no wrong place to begin, and you can return home at any time.

## 0:43–1:06 — Navigate the site

**Picture:** At desktop width, point to the navigation bar and move from left to right. Then resize below the navigation breakpoint, click the menu button, and show the open menu. Select “Take My Next Step.”

**On-screen text:**

> Use the top menu — or tap ☰ on a smaller screen.

**Narration:**

> On a wide screen, use the menu across the top. On a phone, tablet, or most laptops, select the menu button to see every page. The highlighted item tells you where you are. You can also use the links at the bottom of every page.

## 1:06–1:31 — Take My Next Step

**Picture:** Show the Take My Next Step page hero, then scroll slowly through its choices. Pause over links for enrolled students and students who are not sure.

**On-screen text:**

> 🏫 Find your next school step

**Narration:**

> Take My Next Step helps you move toward the right school or program. It has guidance for students who are already enrolled and students who are still deciding. Follow the section that fits you, complete one action, and come back for the next one.

## 1:31–1:56 — Credits and records

**Picture:** Use the navigation to open Credits. Show the quick-action cards and credit information. Then open Records and show the transcript-transfer steps.

**On-screen text:**

> 🔢 Know your credits  •  📄 Find your records

**Narration:**

> The Credits page helps you understand what you have finished, what is in progress, and what you may still need for graduation. The Records page explains how to request and send transcripts. Records matter because every completed credit should follow you to your next school.

## 1:56–2:20 — Explore strengths and costs

**Picture:** Open YouScience and pause on the results and career-planning content. Then open Pay for Future and show the linked category buttons without lingering on personal contact details.

**On-screen text:**

> 🧠 Explore strengths  •  💰 Plan how to pay

**Narration:**

> Your YouScience results can help you notice strengths and careers you may not have considered. Pay for My Future can help you explore financial aid, scholarships, apprenticeships, and other support. Cost is an important question, but you do not have to answer it alone.

## 2:20–2:53 — Why finish high school?

**Picture:** Return to the home page, pause on “Graduation,” then show the Adult Education page. Use a gentle transition to a clean text card with the key phrases below.

**On-screen text:**

> A diploma can help open doors to:
> - jobs and promotions
> - training and apprenticeships
> - college
> - long-term goals

**Narration:**

> Continuing your education and earning a high school diploma can open more doors. Many employers, apprenticeships, training programs, and colleges ask for a diploma or an accepted equivalent. Finishing also shows that you can keep going through challenges, learn new skills, and complete a long-term goal. If a traditional high school is not the right fit, the Adult Education page can help you compare other paths.

## 2:53–3:34 — More than one good path

**Picture:** Open My Future. Show the path cards, then scroll to Technical School, 2-Year College, and 4-Year College as each is discussed.

**On-screen text:**

> 🔧 Technical training<br>
> 🎓 2-year college<br>
> 🎓 4-year college

**Narration:**

> After high school, there is more than one good path. Technical training is hands-on and focused on skills for a specific kind of work. A two-year college can lead to a certificate or associate degree, or help you prepare to transfer. A four-year college can lead to a bachelor's degree and careers that require one. Compare the time, cost, support, and result of each choice. The best path is the one that fits your goals and circumstances.

## 3:34–3:55 — Resources

**Picture:** Open Resources. Show the “On This Page” table of contents, expand it at a narrow width, and select one category. Scroll just enough to demonstrate that the link moves to that section.

**On-screen text:**

> Find support for school — and life outside school.

**Narration:**

> The Resources page connects you with help for school and life outside school, including housing, food, transportation, jobs, identification, health, clothing, and more. Use On This Page to jump directly to the kind of support you need.

## 3:55–4:20 — Ask for help and close

**Picture:** Open Get Help. Pause on the page heading and contact cards. Finish by returning to the home-page message, “You just need to know your next step.”

**On-screen text:**

> You do not have to do this alone.<br>
> Visit My Next Step – YESS<br>
> ktaylordac.github.io/my-next-step-yess/

**Narration:**

> If you get stuck, open Get Help. YESS staff can help you understand credits, records, school choices, training, and college planning. Asking for help is a strong next step. Keep learning, keep asking questions, and keep moving forward. You do not have to know your whole plan. You just need to know your next step.

## Editing notes

- Show real page interactions rather than a fast montage. Each click should be easy to follow.
- Use straight cuts or short dissolves. Avoid rapid motion, flashing effects, or loud sound effects.
- Keep page headings visible long enough to read.
- When demonstrating the responsive menu, do not crop out the menu button.
- If background music is used, lower it under speech and fade it out before the final sentence.
- Use the supplied captions rather than relying only on YouTube's automatic captions.
