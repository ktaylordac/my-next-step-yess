⭐ Important School Supports

These programs may be especially helpful for students and families during school transitions.

💳 Fee Waiver

Qualifying students may be able to have certain school fees waived. Granite School District information says families generally have 30 school days from the first day of school to apply; students who enroll after the year begins generally have 30 school days from registration. A family experiencing a qualifying financial emergency generally has 30 school days from the event to apply.

Supporting income documentation may be required. Apply through the parent portal during registration or use the Utah State Board of Education school-fee information and forms.

🛠️ Job Corps

Want career training, a high school diploma or equivalent, and support as you prepare for work? Job Corps is a free education and career-training program for eligible young adults, generally ages 16–24.

🎓 Finish High School

Eligible students may work toward a high school diploma or equivalent while completing career training.

🛠️ Learn a Career

Job Corps offers training in areas such as construction, automotive, health care, information technology, transportation, hospitality, advanced manufacturing, and more. Not every center offers every program.

Job Corps has residential and nonresidential options. Benefits can include housing, meals, basic medical care, books and supplies, training clothing, and a living allowance for eligible students.

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 If You Are in Foster Care

“I don't know who can help me.”

Ask your caseworker, foster parent/guardian, school counselor, Education Training Advocate, or Youth-in-Care contact. You can also ask Kristen at YESS for help finding the right person.

“I am worried about school when I move.”

Ask who your new school counselor is, where your records are going, how many credits you have, and what your next education plan will be. Utah Youth-in-Care rules require education programs to support educational continuity when students move between placements.

“I want to go to college or technical school.”

Ask about FAFSA, scholarships, ETV funds, the TAL Scholarship, and Utah college or career-training programs. DCFS maintains education and employment resources specifically for youth transitioning from care.

“I need help after I leave foster care.”

Ask about Transition to Adult Living (TAL) services and your local TAL coordinator. DCFS lists current regional contacts on its Youth Services page.

🧡 You still have options.

Being in foster care, moving placements, or being between schools does not mean you have to put your education on hold. Ask questions, keep your credit information, and make sure your next school knows what you have already completed.

ASK KRISTEN FOR HELP

📚 Your education should move with you.

When you move to treatment, secure care, or another placement, ask these four questions:

  1. Who is my teacher or education contact?
  2. How many credits do I have?
  3. What credits can I work on now?
  4. How will my records and credits move when I leave?

Utah rules require education programs serving Youth in Care to coordinate educational services and transition planning when students move between placements.
