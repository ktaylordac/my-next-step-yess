🧩 Options After High School

Your IEP can help you plan for adult life. Explore every kind of Utah option, then ask your IEP team which programs fit your goals and eligibility.

Start with your IEP team

If you have not earned a regular high school diploma, you may be eligible for school-district transition services. Your IEP team decides what services you need and documents them in your transition plan. Ask before you exit high school—graduation can end your eligibility for school-based IEP services.

🏫 Utah school-district transition programs

These programs usually help eligible students ages 18–22 practice employment, transportation, community access, independent living, and self-advocacy. Placement is an IEP-team decision, not an automatic enrollment.

Granite Transition Services Academy (GTSA)

GTSA is Granite School District’s post-high transition option. Ask your YESS case manager or IEP team whether GTSA matches your goals and what you need to do before leaving high school.

  • For eligible Granite students with an IEP
  • Individual transition goals and community-based learning
  • Employment and adult-life preparation

Live in another Utah school district?

Most districts have a transition contact or post-high program, but names and eligibility rules differ. Contact your resident district’s special education office and ask: “What 18–22 transition program serves my address, and can my IEP team make a referral?”

🌟 All kinds of post-high-school options

You can combine options. For example, you might attend a district transition program while building job skills, then move into work or college.

🎓 College or university

You can apply to a degree or certificate program and request disability accommodations. Colleges follow disability law, but they do not continue your high-school IEP. You must contact the school’s accessibility office and provide the documents it requests.

Utah public colleges and universities →

🧠 Inclusive college programs

Some programs are designed for students with intellectual or developmental disabilities and include classes, campus life, work experience, and independent-living skills. Search the statewide directory and ask each program about admission, cost, housing, and financial aid.

Utah technical colleges offer hands-on certificates for specific careers. Ask the accessibility office what accommodations are available before you enroll.

💼 Work and job coaching

Vocational Rehabilitation may help eligible people prepare for, find, and keep a job. Services can include counseling, training, assistive technology, and supported employment.

🏠 Independent-living support

Utah’s Centers for Independent Living help people with disabilities build skills, use community resources, advocate for themselves, and plan for more independence.

🤝 Long-term disability services

The Division of Services for People with Disabilities (DSPD) may provide or fund supports for eligible Utahns. Waiting lists can be long, so ask about applying before high school ends.

✅ Build your plan before you leave school

Bring this list to your next IEP meeting. You do not have to figure it out alone.

Ask your IEP team

  • Am I on track for a regular diploma, and when will my IEP eligibility end?
  • Would a district 18–22 transition program fit my goals?
  • Can I visit a program before making a decision?
  • What work experiences and travel training can I try now?
  • Who will help connect me with adult agencies?

Keep your documents

  • Your most recent IEP and evaluations
  • Your Summary of Performance when you exit
  • School transcript, ID, and Social Security card
  • Names and contact information for your support team
  • Applications and eligibility letters from adult agencies

Want help choosing?

Your YESS counselor and IEP team can help you compare programs, contact your resident district, and prepare questions for a visit.
