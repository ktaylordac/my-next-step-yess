FREE CAREER TRAINING

🦺 Meta’s America’s Workforce Academy

Meta’s program offers free training for entry-level skilled-trades jobs building and maintaining data centers. The program is designed to connect graduates with participating employers, but a job is not automatic: you still need to finish the training and meet the employer’s hiring requirements.

What you need to apply

  • ☐ Be at least 18 years old.
  • ☐ Have a high school diploma or GED.
  • ☐ Be authorized to work in the United States.
  • ☐ Be able to take part in the full training schedule and do the physical work required for the trade.
  • ☐ Meet any location, transportation, safety, drug-screening, or background-check rules listed for your training site or employer.

What if you have a juvenile justice or legal history?

A past juvenile justice or legal-system involvement is not listed as an automatic reason you cannot apply. You may still submit the interest form. However, a training site or participating employer may review your background and apply its own hiring or job-site rules. Meta does not promise that every applicant will be accepted or hired.

If you are unsure, apply and ask the program about your situation before sharing more information than the application requires. Juvenile records, sealed records, and adult convictions can be treated differently.

🌟 If you have been in foster care or juvenile justice

You may qualify for extra help. This can include filing the FAFSA without parent information, education and training vouchers, tuition help, and scholarships. Your history does not disqualify you from college aid. Answer the FAFSA questions based on what has actually happened to you, and ask a financial aid office to review your situation.

📝 FAFSA and independent student questions

The FAFSA is free. It is the main application for federal Pell Grants, work-study, federal student loans, and much state and college aid.

Start the FAFSA

Create your StudentAid.gov account, choose the correct school year, and list every school you may attend. Never pay a website to file the FAFSA.

Complete the official FAFSA →

FAFSA help for unusual circumstances →

Where to report foster care or court status

In the Student Personal Circumstances section, look for the dependency-status questions. Answer “Yes” if, at any time since age 13, you were in foster care or were a dependent or ward of the court. This can make you an independent student, so parent information is not required.

Being on probation or having a juvenile court case by itself is not always the same as being a ward of the court. If you are unsure, ask the college financial aid office. You can also report homelessness, no safe contact with parents, or other unusual circumstances in this part of the FAFSA and ask the school to review documentation.

💼 Employer help

Some employers pay part of your tuition, books, certificates, or job training.

Ask before you accept a job

  • When can I start using the education benefit?
  • Which schools and programs qualify?
  • Does the employer pay first, or reimburse me later?
  • What grades and work hours do I need?
  • Would I owe money back if I leave the job?

✅ Before you choose a program

  • ☐ Is the school or training provider approved for the aid I want to use?
  • ☐ What is the full cost, including tools, fees, transportation, and housing?
  • ☐ How long will it take?
  • ☐ What credential will I earn?
  • ☐ What jobs does it prepare me for?
  • ☐ Which grants and scholarships are confirmed in writing?
  • ☐ What will I need to pay or borrow myself?
